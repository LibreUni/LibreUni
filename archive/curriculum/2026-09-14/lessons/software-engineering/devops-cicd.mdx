# Delivery Pipelines and Operational Feedback

DevOps is a socio-technical approach to reducing the delay and risk between a change and reliable operation. A pipeline is not a badge for using YAML; it is a repeatable chain of evidence and controlled actions with owners, failure visibility, and recovery.

## CI, delivery, deployment

Continuous integration keeps changes integrated and checked frequently. Continuous delivery keeps a releasable artifact available through automated build/test/release steps, while a human or policy may approve production. Continuous deployment automatically promotes every change that meets the policy. The distinction is control and evidence, not marketing vocabulary.

## Pipeline as a risk map

A useful pipeline may contain:

1. reproducible source checkout and dependency resolution;
2. formatting, static analysis, unit and property tests;
3. integration/contract tests at database and provider boundaries;
4. security and license checks with reviewed policy;
5. artifact creation, signing/provenance, and retention;
6. migration rehearsal and compatibility checks;
7. staged deployment with health and business metrics; and
8. promotion, rollback, or forward-repair decisions.

Do not hide a flaky or advisory check inside a required gate. Label it, assign an owner, and track whether it should become reliable enough to block.

Metrics need a time window, sample population, baseline, and action threshold. A health endpoint that returns 200 while business writes fail is not sufficient evidence.

## Release and recovery

Blue-green and canary releases reduce blast radius but add traffic-routing, state compatibility, and rollback complexity. A database migration may make rollback impossible; use additive changes, dual reads/writes only with a sunset plan, and forward repair when required. Keep secrets in a controlled secret store, restrict log fields, and prevent credentials from entering artifacts or test fixtures.

Observability should connect technical and user outcomes: error rate, latency percentiles, queue age, migration state, authorization failures, and domain invariants. A runbook says how to detect, contain, preserve evidence, recover, and verify. “Restart the service” is not a recovery plan for corrupted data.

4. Design a canary stop condition for duplicate notifications.

### Diagnostic solutions

1. Resolve/pin the dependency, run vulnerability/license policy, regression and integration tests, produce provenance, and require review for exceptions.
2. An old binary may be redeployed while a schema or external side effect remains changed; repair may require forward migration, reconciliation, or restore.
3. Successful HTTP requests can still create wrong totals, duplicate effects, stale data, or unauthorized reads; instrument domain outcomes.
4. Define a baseline and statistically/operationally meaningful threshold over a window; pause promotion, preserve samples, and investigate idempotency/provider changes.
