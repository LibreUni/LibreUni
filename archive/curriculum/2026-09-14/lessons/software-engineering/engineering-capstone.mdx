# Software Engineering Capstone: Evolve a Safety-Critical Service

The capstone is an engineering argument backed by executable evidence. You will evolve a small service that schedules medication reminders for a care team. The service is intentionally modest: the difficulty is making its guarantees explicit, preserving them while requirements change, and showing what the system does when dependencies fail.

## Product contract

The service accepts a request to schedule a reminder for a patient, medication, local time, and recurrence rule. A reminder may be delivered at most once for a scheduled occurrence, but a provider may retry a delivery request. A caregiver may cancel a future occurrence; cancellation must not erase the audit record. The system must never silently invent a dose, send a reminder after a recorded cancellation, or expose one patient's data to another care team.

Write the contract in a form another engineer can test. Define the identity of an occurrence, the time zone and daylight-saving policy, the meaning of a missed delivery, the maximum tolerated delay, and the behavior when the notification provider is unavailable. Distinguish hard safety invariants from service-level objectives. “Reliable” is not a requirement until the team specifies the event, denominator, window, and evidence.

## Specification and design evidence

Submit a short specification containing:

- preconditions, postconditions, and invariants for scheduling, cancellation, dispatch, and audit;
- a state model for an occurrence, including legal and illegal transitions;
- examples and counterexamples for time zones, duplicate requests, provider timeouts, and authorization;
- an explicit non-goal list; and
- a traceability table linking each outcome to a test, review artifact, or operational check.

Use a domain model that keeps policy separate from infrastructure. A pure recurrence calculator should not open a database connection. A repository should not decide whether a caregiver is authorized. A dispatcher should coordinate retries without becoming the source of truth for occurrence state. The boundaries are hypotheses: defend them with change scenarios and failure analysis, not pattern names.

## A small executable seam

The following pure function is deliberately incomplete. It defines one seam that can be tested without a clock, database, network, or framework. Extend it only after writing the cases that determine the policy.

The runner is not the capstone implementation. It is a fast feedback surface for the policy boundary. The submitted service must add persistence, authorization, provider idempotency, observability, and failure recovery without changing the meaning of the pure decision.

## Verification and evolution

Build a test portfolio, not a test-count trophy. Include:

1. example-based unit tests for each legal state transition;
2. property tests for occurrence identity, cancellation monotonicity, and duplicate delivery requests;
3. integration tests against a real persistence boundary or a faithful test container;
4. contract tests for the notification provider and the public API;
5. a concurrency test for two dispatch workers claiming one occurrence; and
6. a regression test for every defect discovered during the project.

For each test, state what defect it could detect and what it cannot establish. A green suite that never exercises a crash between commit and provider call does not establish delivery safety. A mock that returns only happy-path responses does not establish the provider contract.

Refactor in small, behavior-preserving steps. Before each structural change, record the characterization tests that constrain it. Afterward, compare the public behavior, mutation-test a representative rule, and inspect the diff for accidental contract changes. If a design pattern appears, explain the pressure that caused it and the cost it introduces; a pattern is not evidence of quality by itself.

## Delivery and operations

Provide a reproducible pipeline that performs formatting, static analysis, unit tests, integration tests, dependency checks, and an artifact build. Separate required gates from advisory reports. Pin action/tool versions, produce machine-readable results, and make a failed gate actionable.

The deployment plan must state:

- how configuration and secrets enter the process without appearing in logs;
- how database migrations are expanded, observed, and rolled back or repaired;
- how a canary is judged and stopped;
- which metrics expose scheduling lag, duplicate provider requests, authorization failures, and queue age;
- how an operator reconstructs one occurrence from audit records; and
- how the team restores service after a bad release or corrupted queue.

## Deliverables and assessment

Submit a repository that a reviewer can build from a clean checkout, plus a design report of at most 2,500 words. The report must include the contract, domain/state model, architecture decision records, traceability matrix, threat and failure model, test strategy, measurement evidence, deployment plan, and deliberate omissions.

Assessment is based on:

- semantic correctness and explicit invariants (25%);
- tests that demonstrate transfer, failure handling, and concurrency (25%);
- design trade-offs and maintainability evidence (20%);
- delivery, observability, and recovery evidence (20%); and
- clarity, reproducibility, and professional responsibility (10%).

Full credit requires a counterexample for each major rejected alternative. A polished diagram without executable evidence is incomplete; a large test suite without a precise contract is also incomplete. Do not claim exactly-once behavior when the system only provides idempotent effects under a documented provider contract.

## Deliberate omissions

This project does not teach clinical dosage policy, distributed consensus, real payment processing, or production cloud administration. Those domains require specialist review and separate safety controls. The capstone models the engineering reasoning needed to make boundaries visible; it is not authorization to deploy a medical system.
