# Observer and Strategy: Events and Variation

Observer separates a subject from interested listeners; Strategy separates an algorithm choice from its caller. Both reduce direct coupling, but Observer introduces delivery and lifecycle problems, while Strategy demands substitutable algorithms and comparable result semantics.

## Observer lifecycle

An in-process subject can deliver synchronously, queue asynchronously, or persist an event for later consumers. Synchronous delivery preserves ordering but lets a slow listener block the publisher and can create reentrant mutation. Asynchronous delivery requires queue capacity, retry/idempotency, shutdown, and observability. Unsubscription must not race with an in-flight callback without a defined rule.

## Strategy contract

A Strategy should express one variation axis—sorting, pricing, retry, or routing—and expose a common contract. Document valid inputs, output equivalence, stability/tie behavior, complexity expectations, and failure modes. A strategy that silently uses different units or precision is not substitutable merely because its method signature matches.

### Diagnostic solutions

1. Bound the queue, reject or spill events, expose lag, retry by policy, and define shutdown/drain behavior.
2. A listener mutates the subject while it is iterating listeners, causing invalidation, recursion, or an unexpected event order.
3. Nonnegative tax, one currency/rounding contract, and no mutation of the source order; add jurisdiction/unsupported behavior.
4. It enables deduplication, tracing, audit correlation, and safe retries.
