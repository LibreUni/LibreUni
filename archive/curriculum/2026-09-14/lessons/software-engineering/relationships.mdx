# Object Relationships and Ownership

A line between two classes is not a design. A relationship must answer whether objects may exist independently, who owns the association, how many partners are allowed, how navigation works, and what happens when one side is deleted or unavailable.

## Association and multiplicity

An association may be one-to-one, one-to-many, or many-to-many, but multiplicity is a constraint, not decoration. “A course has 0..* enrollments” differs from “each enrollment belongs to exactly one course.” Optionality, uniqueness, temporal validity, and authorization may all be part of the relationship contract.

## Aggregation, composition, and external ownership

Use composition when a part's identity and lifetime are owned by the aggregate boundary, such as an order line that has no independent meaning outside an order in a particular model. Use a plain association when both objects have independent lifetimes, such as a user and an organization membership managed by a separate policy. “Aggregation” is often too vague to be useful; say who owns what instead.

## Navigation and coupling

Bidirectional navigation can make invariants easier to maintain but increases coupling and synchronization work. A child that stores a parent reference and a parent that stores a mutable child collection need one update operation that keeps both sides consistent. If references cross a service boundary, the relationship is a protocol or identifier, not an in-memory pointer.

## Transfer exercises

1. State the deletion rule for a shopping cart and its line items.
2. Give a many-to-many relationship that needs attributes on the association itself.
3. Explain why a database foreign key does not automatically define object ownership.
4. Design a test for a one-to-one relationship with replacement.

### Diagnostic solutions

1. Decide whether deleting a cart deletes lines, whether lines are reusable, and what happens to historical prices. Encode and test the decision.
2. Student-course enrollment needs term, grade, and status; model enrollment as an entity/value with two references.
3. A foreign key enforces reference integrity; it does not specify lifetime, authorization, retention, or which service may mutate either row.
4. Create A linked to B, replace with C, assert uniqueness and the old B's state according to the contract, then test concurrent replacement if relevant.
