This recap sheet synthesises the foundational results of integral calculus developed across Chapter 2. It consolidates Darboux partitions, the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, geometric accumulation, critical failure modes when hypotheses fail, and standard analytical pitfalls.



## Definitions and Results

### 1. Darboux Partitions and Integrability

* **Partitions and Darboux Sums** (<Ref id="partition-definition" />): A partition $P = \{x_0, x_1, \dots, x_n\}$ subdivides $[a, b]$ into $n$ subintervals $[x_{i-1}, x_i]$ of length $\Delta x_i = x_i - x_{i-1}$. For bounded $f$, the lower and upper Darboux sums are $L(f, P) = \sum_{i=1}^n m_i \Delta x_i$ and $U(f, P) = \sum_{i=1}^n M_i \Delta x_i$, where $m_i = \inf_{[x_{i-1}, x_i]} f$ and $M_i = \sup_{[x_{i-1}, x_i]} f$.
* **Riemann Integrability** (<Ref id="riemann-integrable" />): The lower and upper Darboux integrals are $L(f) = \sup_P L(f, P)$ and $U(f) = \inf_P U(f, P)$. A bounded function $f$ is Riemann integrable on $[a, b]$ if and only if $L(f) = U(f)$. The common value is denoted $\int_a^b f(x)\,dx$.
* **Continuous Functions are Integrable** (<Ref id="continuous-integrable" />): Every continuous function $f \in C([a, b])$ is Riemann integrable. Proof relies on uniform continuity on compact $[a, b]$ ensuring oscillation $\omega(f, [x_{i-1}, x_i]) < \frac{\varepsilon}{b - a}$ for sufficiently fine partitions.

### 2. Properties and the Fundamental Theorem

* **Mean Value Theorem for Integrals** (<Ref id="mvt-integrals" />): If $f \in C([a, b])$, there exists at least one $c \in [a, b]$ such that $\int_a^b f(x)\,dx = f(c)(b - a)$. Geometrically, the integral equals the area of a rectangle of base $b - a$ and height $f(c)$.
* **Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, Part 1** (<Ref id="ftc-part-1" />): If $f$ is Riemann integrable on $[a, b]$ and $F(x) = \int_a^x f(t)\,dt$, then $F$ is continuous on $[a, b]$. Furthermore, if $f$ is continuous at $x_0 \in (a, b)$, then $F$ is differentiable at $x_0$ with $F'(x_0) = f(x_0)$.
* **Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, Part 2** (<Ref id="ftc-part-2" />): If $f$ is Riemann integrable on $[a, b]$ and $F$ is any antiderivative of $f$ on $[a, b]$ (i.e., $F'(x) = f(x)$ for all $x \in (a, b)$ with $F \in C([a, b])$), then $\int_a^b f(x)\,dx = F(b) - F(a)$.

### 3. Applications and Geometric Accumulation

* **Volume by Slicing** (<Ref id="volume-by-slices" />): For a solid whose cross-sectional area perpendicular to the $x$-axis is given by continuous $A(x)$ for $x \in [a, b]$, the exact volume is the accumulation $V = \int_a^b A(x)\,dx$. For solids of revolution rotated about the $x$-axis, this yields the disk method $A(x) = \pi [R(x)]^2$ or washer method $A(x) = \pi ([R(x)]^2 - [r(x)]^2)$.
* **Arc Length of a Smooth Curve** (<Ref id="arc-length" />): If $f'$ is continuous on $[a, b]$, the total rectilinear arc length of $y = f(x)$ is the limit of polygonal secant lengths: $L = \int_a^b \sqrt{1 + [f'(x)]^2}\,dx$.

## Method Chooser

| Goal / Problem Type | Standard Technique | Key Steps |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Prove a function $f$ is integrable** | Darboux Oscillation (<Ref id="partition-definition" />) | For arbitrary $\varepsilon > 0$, construct a partition $P$ bounding total oscillation $\sum (M_i - m_i)\Delta x_i < \varepsilon$. |
| **Differentiate $\int_{u(x)}^{v(x)} f(t)\,dt$** | <Ref id="ftc-part-1" /> + Leibniz rule | Split at constant $c$: $\int_c^{v(x)} f - \int_c^{u(x)} f$. Apply chain rule: $v'(x)f(v(x)) - u'(x)f(u(x))$. |
| **Evaluate definite integral $\int_a^b f(x)\,dx$** | <Ref id="ftc-part-2" /> | Find antiderivative $F$ such that $F' = f$. Evaluate $F(b) - F(a)$. Check integrand is continuous across $[a, b]$. |
| **Bound unknown integral $\int_a^b f(x)g(x)\,dx$** | Monotonicity & MVT (<Ref id="mvt-integrals" />) | If $m \le f(x) \le M$ and $g \ge 0$, then $m \int g \le \int fg \le M \int g$. Apply Cauchy-Schwarz or MVT for integrals. |
| **Compute volume of solid of revolution** | Slicing / Disks (<Ref id="volume-by-slices" />) | Determine cross-sectional slice area $A(x) = \pi [R(x)]^2$ and integrate along rotational axis $V = \int_a^b A(x)\,dx$. |
| **Compute arc length of graph $y = f(x)$** | Rectification (<Ref id="arc-length" />) | Evaluate $L = \int_a^b \sqrt{1 + [f'(x)]^2}\,dx$ after verifying $f'$ is continuous on $[a, b]$. |



## Hypothesis Checklist

* **Riemann Integrability (<Ref id="riemann-integrable" />)**
  * *Boundedness is essential:* If $f(x) = 1/x$ for $x \in (0, 1]$ and $f(0) = 0$, then on the first subinterval $[0, x_1]$, $M_1 = \infty$. The upper Darboux sum does not exist as a finite real number.
  * *Countably dense discontinuities:* Dirichlet's function $f = \mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{Q}}$ is bounded on $[0, 1]$, but $U(f, P) = 1$ and $L(f, P) = 0$ for every partition $P$; $U(f) = 1 \neq 0 = L(f)$, failing integrability.

* **Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, Part 1 (<Ref id="ftc-part-1" />)**
  * *Continuity of integrand is required for differentiability of $F$:* Let $f(x) = \operatorname{sgn}(x)$ on $[-1, 1]$. $f$ is integrable, and $F(x) = \int_{-1}^x f(t)\,dt = |x| - 1$ is continuous. However, $F$ is not differentiable at $x = 0$ because $f$ has a jump discontinuity at $0$.

* **Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, Part 2 (<Ref id="ftc-part-2" />)**
  * *Antiderivative must exist on the entire closed interval $[a, b]$:* Let $f(x) = \frac{1}{x^2}$ on $[-1, 1]$. Naively using the algebraic antiderivative $F(x) = -\frac{1}{x}$ gives $F(1) - F(-1) = -1 - (1) = -2$. But $f(x) > 0$ everywhere, so an integral cannot be negative! The error is that $f$ is unbounded and discontinuous at $x = 0$; $F$ is not defined on $[-1, 1]$.

* **Mean Value Theorem for Integrals (<Ref id="mvt-integrals" />)**
  * *Continuity of $f$ is required to attain the mean value:* Let $f(x) = 0$ on $[0, 1)$ and $f(1) = 2$. Then $\int_0^1 f(x)\,dx = 0$, giving average value $0$, which is attained at $x \in (0, 1)$. But if $f(x) = 1$ on $[0, 1]$ and $f(x) = 3$ on $(1, 2]$, the average is $\frac{1 + 3}{2} = 2$. Yet $f(x)$ never takes the value $2$.



## Typical Errors

1. **Blind Antiderivative Evaluation Across Singularities:** Computing $\int_{-1}^1 \frac{1}{x}\,dx = [\ln|x|]_{-1}^1 = 0 - 0 = 0$. The integrand is unbounded and discontinuous at $x = 0$; the integral diverges.
2. **Missing Chain Rule in Accumulation Derivatives:** Writing $\frac{d}{dx} \int_0^{x^2} \sin(t)\,dt = \sin(x^2)$ instead of $2x \sin(x^2)$. By the chain rule, $\frac{d}{dx} F(u(x)) = u'(x) F'(u(x))$.
3. **Omitting Absolute Values in Area Integrals:** Integrating $\int_a^b (f(x) - g(x))\,dx$ without verifying which curve is on top. If curves cross at $c \in (a, b)$, the positive and negative lobes partially cancel, underestimating the geometric area.
4. **Confusing Darboux Sums with Darboux Integrals:** $U(f, P)$ depends on a specific partition $P$. The upper integral $U(f) = \inf_P U(f, P)$ is a unique intrinsic real number.
5. **Swapping Limits Without Sign Inversion:** Forgetting that reversing integration limits inverts the sign: $\int_b^a f(x)\,dx = -\int_a^b f(x)\,dx$.
